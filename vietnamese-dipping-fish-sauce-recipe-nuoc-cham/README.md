## Nuoc Cham Meaning and Pronunciation: A Short Vietnamese Sauce Glossary

Nuoc cham means "dipping sauce" in Vietnamese: nước is water or liquid, and chấm is to dip. It is pronounced roughly "nuh-uhk chum", with both syllables in a rising tone. On English-language menus, the name usually refers to the sweet-sour fish sauce dip served with Vietnamese food.

### What Does Nuoc Cham Mean?

Read literally, nước chấm is "dipping liquid". In Vietnam, the term covers any dipping sauce, from soy-based dips to fermented shrimp paste. Outside Vietnam, it has narrowed to one sauce: fish sauce mixed with lime, sugar, water, garlic and chili. Vietnamese cooks often call that specific version nước mắm pha, which means "mixed fish sauce".

### How Do You Pronounce Nuoc Cham?

Say nước as "nuh-uhk", one quick syllable that starts with an unrounded "uh" and ends on a clipped "k". Say chấm as "chum", with a short vowel and closed lips at the end. Both words carry the rising tone (dấu sắc), so the pitch lifts slightly on each. Southern speakers tend to soften the vowels, while Northern speakers keep them sharper.

### Common Spellings

Without Vietnamese diacritics, the name appears as nuoc cham or nuoc mam cham, the second spelling stressing the fish sauce base. With diacritics, the correct form is nước chấm. English recipes and menus mostly drop the diacritics, so both forms refer to the same sauce.
